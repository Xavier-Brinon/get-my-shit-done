*Template block (=<template>=):*
The skeleton must cover "Your First Phase" --- one complete GMSD cycle:
=discuss-phase= then =plan-phase= then =execute-phase=.

Required structural elements (all locked decisions from CONTEXT.org):
- "What you'll learn" section at the top (bookend 1 --- sets expectations
  with bullet list of concrete outcomes)
- Prerequisites section (GMSD installed, a project to work on)
- Step 1: Discuss your first phase (=discuss-phase= command)
- Step 2: Plan the phase (=plan-phase= command)
- Step 3: Execute the plan (=execute-phase= command)
- Milestone acknowledgements between major steps --- warm, encouraging tone
  (e.g., "Great --- you've just created your first plan!")
- "What you learned" section at the bottom (bookend 2 --- echoes the
  "What you'll learn" bullets in past tense, encouraging close)

*Tone:* Warm, encouraging, congratulatory at milestones. Steps are
unconditional (teacher-led, no branching). Steps chosen to build
understanding, not for efficiency.

*Guidance text:* Use org-mode comments (=# comment= for single-line,
=#+begin_comment= / =#+end_comment= for multi-line) to explain each
section's Diataxis purpose. These are invisible when exported, visible
when editing.

*Anti-patterns to avoid:*
- Do NOT use backticks anywhere --- use =~code~= or ==verbatim==
- Do NOT use conditional language ("if you want to...") --- tutorial steps
  are unconditional
- Do NOT explain theory inline --- defer rationale to comments or omit
- Do NOT mix how-to framing ("when you need to...") into the tutorial

*Good examples block (=<good_examples>=):*
One fully worked example showing the completed tutorial for a realistic
but generic project (e.g., a personal task tracker or similar). This is
at Claude's Discretion per CONTEXT.org. The example should demonstrate
what a filled-in tutorial looks like from start to finish.

*Guidelines block (=<guidelines>=):*
Authoring guidance for agents generating tutorials. Include the Diataxis
disambiguation test: "Does the reader know what they want before they
start? If yes, they want a how-to. If no, they want this tutorial."

=-= as bullet character, =~code~= / ==verbatim== markup, max 4 heading
levels, title case for =*= headings, sentence case for =**= and below,
blank line before every heading.

Create =templates/howto.org= following the meta-template block pattern.

*File-level property drawer:*
- =:diataxis_primary: how-to=
- =:diataxis_secondary: none=
- =:diataxis_rationale:= explaining that the reader follows the generated
  how-to to accomplish a specific operational goal

*Template block (=<template>=):*
A generic repeatable-procedure skeleton (locked decision: NOT scoped to
GMSD specifically). Must be usable for any multi-step procedure: GMSD
workflows, deployment runbooks, operational guides.

Required structural elements (all locked decisions from CONTEXT.org):
- Goal statement in =#+title:= and =*= heading (stated twice --- How-to
  rule: confirm scope immediately, e.g., "How to [Goal]")
- Prerequisites section --- list only blockers, skip anything the reader
  can infer. Short.
- Numbered steps section --- imperative verbs, concrete actions. Linear
  sequence. Inline conditional notes for variations (e.g., "If using
  Docker, substitute X") but NO branching paths.
- Verification section --- what success looks like, with a bash command
  block and expected output
- Troubleshooting section --- brief symptom-to-fix pattern only. Keep as
  generic placeholders in the skeleton.

*Tone:* Professional, concise, respects the reader's time. Neutral
third-person. NO warm/encouraging language (that is tutorial tone only).

*Guidance text:* Use org-mode comments (=# comment= for single-line,
=#+begin_comment= / =#+end_comment= for multi-line) to explain each
section's purpose. These are invisible when exported, visible when editing.

*Anti-patterns to avoid:*
- Do NOT use backticks --- use =~code~= or ==verbatim==
- Do NOT include background theory or explanations of "why" --- how-to
  assumes competence
- Do NOT use encouraging/congratulatory language --- that is tutorial tone
- Do NOT scope the skeleton to GMSD specifically --- keep generic

*Good examples block (=<good_examples>=):*
One fully worked example showing a completed how-to. Per CONTEXT.org this
is at Claude's Discretion. The example should demonstrate a realistic
procedure (can be GMSD-specific in the example, since the skeleton itself
is generic).

*Guidelines block (=<guidelines>=):*
Authoring guidance for agents generating how-to documents. Include the
Diataxis disambiguation test: "Does this help the reader reach the goal
faster? If it explains rather than directs, remove it or move it to a
comment."

Follow org-styleguide.md conventions throughout: 78-char prose wrap,
=-= as bullet character, =~code~= / ==verbatim== markup, max 4 heading
levels, title case for =*= headings, sentence case for =**= and below,
blank line before every heading.
